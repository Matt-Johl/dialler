Why Dialler

iOS suspends apps. Most softphones stop ringing.

A typical SIP softphone

Receives calls only while it is open. Once iOS moves it to the background or closes it, its connection to the PBX lapses and calls no longer reach it.

Dialler

Rings in every state. When a call arrives, the Dialler server wakes the phone through Apple's Local Push Connectivity, and iOS presents the call like any other.

Features

Built for the office phone system

  • Background wake-ups

    Incoming calls wake the app through Local Push Connectivity over your office Wi-Fi. CallKit rings the phone, locked or not.

  • Your PBX, unchanged

    Asterisk, Cisco CUCM or any standard SIP exchange, connected as a trunk or as one registered line per phone. PBX credentials never reach the phone.

  • On-site by design

    Wake-ups, signalling and audio stay on your network. No cloud service, and no reliance on your internet connection. Call history stays on the phone.

  • Encrypted

    TLS signalling and SRTP audio between phone and server. Each phone pins the server's certificate when it enrols.

  • Managed directory

    A contact list for each phone, maintained centrally or imported from CSV. Users can add their own contacts and favourites.

  • Clear audio

    G.722 wideband, with G.711 as a fallback, passed through without transcoding. Voice and signalling traffic carry QoS markings.

Also included: hold, transfer, mute, speaker, keypad and recents.

How it works

A small server beside your PBX

The Dialler server runs on your network. It wakes phones when calls arrive, and it is where you set up, manage and monitor them.

  1. A call arrives

    Your PBX routes it to the Dialler server, as it would to any extension.

  2. The phone wakes

    The server signals the phone through Local Push Connectivity. iOS rings it, whatever state the app is in.

  3. The call connects

    On answer, the app connects to the server, which bridges it to the PBX.

Coverage

Wake-ups work on the Wi-Fi networks you assign to each phone. Away from those networks, the phone does not receive calls. This keeps the entire service on your own infrastructure.

Set-up

Two services, one machine

dialler-server connects to your PBX and wakes phones. dialler-admin provides the web console. Each is a single binary with no runtime dependencies.

7443 TCPWake-ups and signalling
5061 TCPSIP over TLS
8080 TCPEnrolment and directory
20000–20100 UDPCall audio
# the call server
dialler-server \
  -trunk "sip:pbx.office:5060;transport=tcp" \
  -public-host 10.0.0.10 \
  -local-domain office \
  -tls-cert server.pem \
  -tls-key server.key \
  -admin-token-file admin-token

# the web console
dialler-admin \
  -server-ca server.pem \
  -admin-token-file admin-token \
  -password-file password

Connects to the PBX as a SIP trunk. Add -pbx-mode lines to register one line per phone instead.

Management

Every phone, from one console

  • Add a phone

    Assign an extension and a label. The console generates a QR code.

  • Enrol

    Scan the code with the iPhone camera and Dialler configures itself. Codes are single-use and expire after fifteen minutes.

  • Configure

    Set each phone's Wi-Fi networks and directory. Changes reach the phone as soon as they are saved.

  • Replace or revoke

    Revoke a lost phone, or issue a new code for its replacement. The previous credential stops working.

  • Monitor

    See which phones are online, the registration state of each PBX line, and live call quality.

Every change applies only to the phone it names, and never interrupts a call in progress.
